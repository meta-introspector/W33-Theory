# Pass 11231: a dynamical area law for circuits of the substrate's ticks (approximate, not exact)

Producer: `analysis/w33_pass11231_dynamical_area_law.py`
Certificate: `data/w33_pass11231_dynamical_area_law.json`
Regression: `tests/test_w33_pass11231_dynamical_area_law.py`

**Question.** Pass 11226 showed that static networks of the perfect tick p obey the Ryu–Takayanagi law exactly. A
dynamical space-time needs time evolution. Does the entanglement generated by circuits of the geometry's ticks equal
the minimal cut through the circuit's spacetime network?

**Set-up.**
* A chain of 12 qutrits is evolved by brick-wall circuits of depth 10.
* For every interval A of length ≤ 6, at every depth, two quantities are computed:
  * S(A), exactly, in trits (stabilizer ranks);
  * the min-cut through the circuit: worldline segments separating A's outputs from the rest. Initial product states
    are free ends; an initial Bell pair is one node.
* S(A) ≤ min-cut always. This is asserted on every pair.
* Ticks:
  * the perfect tick p;
  * SUM;
  * random two-qutrit Cliffords;
  * random **perfect** gates (p dressed by random single-qutrit Cliffords);
  * the three-qutrit perfect tick VKV, which carries an arrow (A = 2, Pass 11223), in a three-site brick pattern.
* Initial states: |0…0⟩, all-XZ eigenstates, random stabilizer product states, and Bell pairs (the "solvable" states
  of dual-unitary circuits).

## Results (exact on the 570 pairs per run)

| tick \ initial state | \|0…0⟩ | XZ | random product | Bell pairs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| perfect tick p | 15 | 190 | 505 | 479 |
| SUM | 15 | 440 | 226 | 228 |
| random Cliffords | 363 | 444 | 409 | 448 |
| **random perfect gates** | 463 | 444 | 411 | **548** |
| **VKV (three-qutrit)** | 383 | **539** | **534** | 465 |

* **No tick saturates the min-cut everywhere.** The deficits are not only at the open boundary.
* **Closest to an area law:**
  * random perfect gates from Bell pairs, 96%. Their half-chain entropy grows at the maximal rate (1, 3, 3, 5, 5, …) to
    its maximum, 6, as dual-unitary theory predicts.
  * the arrow-carrying three-qutrit tick VKV from product states, 94–95%.
* **The bare perfect tick p is fine-tuned.** It maps Z-type Paulis to Z-type Paulis, so |0…0⟩ never entangles (15/570
  is the depth-zero baseline), and repeating one order-4 gate causes recurrences. Perfection of the gate alone is not
  enough; genericity of the circuit matters.
* SUM, which is not perfect, is the worst entangler from generic states.

## Reading

* Circuits of the geometry's ticks obey an **approximate** dynamical area law.
* Entanglement of a region tracks the minimal spacetime cut within a few percent for perfect gates from solvable
  states, and for the three-qutrit tick that carries the arrow. It is not exact.
* So a dynamical space-time from these circuits is **hosted approximately, not derived**.
* An exact law would need the thermodynamic limit and the solvable-state theory of dual-unitary circuits
  (Bertini, Kos, Prosen; Piroli et al.). Neither is reproduced here.
