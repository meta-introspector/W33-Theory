A second, uniform chiral invariant makes the orientation explicit. The Kashiwara--Maslov index of three product
Lagrangians, two from one split and one from the other, is odd under $\tau$. Oriented by a gauge-invariant profile, its
net chirality is $\mp54$ on the $256$ pair, $\pm18$ on the $6912$ pair and $0$ on every other relation. The index is
the phase of the stabilizer Bargmann invariant, $\tr(P_{L_1}P_{L_2}P_{L_3})\propto(-i)^{\tau(L_1,L_2,L_3)}$ (checked on
random triples). This is the finite-field form of the classical link between the Maslov index and the Weil
representation. The direction of time between two subsystem descriptions is thus carried by a Pancharatnam--Berry
phase. \tierC\ (\cert{w33\_pass11209\_maslov\_chirality.py})

\begin{result}{Theorem 4.8 (the intrinsic arrow for every number of qutrits) \hfill \tierT}
Let $c(S)$ be the largest number of mutually orthogonal $S$-invariant nondegenerate planes: the qutrits a tick can keep
to themselves. For every $n$ and every Clifford tick $S\in\Sp(2n,3)$,
\[
A(S)\;=\;n-c(S),
\qquad
c(S)=\sum_{\lambda=\pm1}\bigl(m_1(\lambda)/2+m_2(\lambda)\bigr)+m_1^{\F_9}(x^2+1),
\]
with $m_s$ the number of Jordan blocks of size $s$. So $A\le n$, $A=n$ exactly when no qutrit is dynamically protected,
and $A\ne1$.
\end{result}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
In any split, $a\le c$ planes are invariant and the rest meet their image at most in a line, so $E\ge n-a\ge n-c$.
Conversely, split off a maximal invariant family and decompose the rest orthogonally by the characteristic polynomial
and the unipotent and unitary Jordan structure. In each indecomposable piece, a Lagrangian for both $\omega$ and
$\omega(\cdot,(S+S^{-1})\cdot)$ that is transverse to its image, or meets it in one eigenvector, yields a split in which
every qutrit meets its own image. Such a Lagrangian comes from a Taussky--Zassenhaus graph, a Cayley-transform parity
subspace, an eigenvector reduction, or a Hermitian real form.
\end{proof}

In every optimal split, each protected qutrit exports nothing and each unprotected one exports \emph{exactly one} of its
two trits. Theorem~4.7 prices a forgotten record at two trits. The best choice of subsystems halves that price: one
trit, or $k_BT\ln3$ by Landauer, per unprotected qutrit per tick. \tierB

The theorem is checked on every conjugacy class for $n\le5$ ($20$, $74$, $278$ and $940$ classes). For large $n$ a random
tick protects $0.7255\ldots$ qutrits on average (exactly $0.725322$, $0.725518$, $0.725535$ for $n=3,4,5$). It has the
maximal arrow with probability about $0.45$, and is arrow-free with probability $P(c=n)$, which is $0.0002$ already at
$n=5$. The same law holds class by class for qubits through $n=5$ and for $5$- and $7$-dits. It is a law of Clifford
dynamics, not a special property of qutrits. The proof covers every odd local dimension; for qubits the law is a
verified conjecture. \tierC\ (\cert{w33\_pass11207\_arrow\_theorem.py}, \cert{w33\_pass11207\_arrow\_by\_planes.py},
\cert{w33\_pass11208\_arrow\_universality.py})

The reflections that carry the maximal time-reversal arrow are also the only \emph{Kramers} time reversals. An
anti-symplectic $\theta$ with $\theta^2=-1$ exists only for an even number of qutrits and is local only by exchanging
them; for two qutrits these are exactly the $36$ reflections of $W(E_6)$. Those with $\theta^2=+1$ are always complex
conjugation in suitable subsystems. \tierT\,\tierC\ (\cert{w33\_pass11210\_kramers\_time\_reversal.py})
