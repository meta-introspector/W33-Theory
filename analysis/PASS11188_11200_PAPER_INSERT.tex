The two pairs of relations that the entanglement data leave tied are mirror images. Complex conjugation acts on phase
space as $\tau\colon(x,z)\mapsto(x,-z)$, the anti-symplectic outer automorphism, and it fixes $16$ of the $20$ relations
and swaps exactly the two tied pairs. So for them the reversed relation \emph{is} the mirrored one, and nothing blind to
the sign of $\omega$ can tell them apart. The Kashiwara--Maslov index of product Lagrangians can: oriented canonically,
its net chirality is $\mp54$ and $\pm18$ on the two pairs and $0$ on the other $16$. It is the phase of the stabilizer
Bargmann invariant, $\tr(P_{L_1}P_{L_2}P_{L_3})\propto(-i)^{\tau(L_1,L_2,L_3)}$. The direction of time between two
subsystem descriptions is recorded in geometric phase and nowhere else.
\tierC\ (\cert{w33\_pass11189\_chiral\_arrow.py})

The two patterns are the only ones. In an $\mathrm{AME}(10,3)$ stabilizer state, the stabilizers trivial on four parties
carry signs whose six-party sign partitions are uniform or $3+3$. The block determinants of every perfect gate are read
off these signs. For three parties the seven local forms are points of the Klein quadric summing to zero, so each sign
graph is one of four types. Only the prism plus an isolated vertex and $K_4+3K_1$ embed in the hyperbolic Klein space.
With an isotropy duality, a SAT computation (two independent solvers) rules out $K_4+3K_1$ and every pattern other than
the two observed. Hence the uniform four-sets of every $\mathrm{AME}(10,3)$ state form a Steiner system $S(3,4,10)$,
\emph{every} perfect five-qutrit Clifford gate is \textsc{C10} or \textsc{cross+perm}, and the $2:5$ ratio of the two
censuses is forced. \tierC\ (\cert{w33\_pass11190\_ame10\_sign\_structure.py})

The arrow of Theorem~4.7 needs a partner to forget, and which qutrit is the partner depends on the subsystem split. For
every tick $S$ and split $F$, forgetting everything but qutrit $q$ destroys $2-\dim(P_q\cap SP_q)$ trits. The
\emph{intrinsic arrow} $A(S)$ is the minimum total over all splits: the loss that no choice of subsystems avoids.

\begin{result}{Theorem 4.8 (the intrinsic arrow) \hfill \tierT}
Let $c(S)$ be the largest number of mutually orthogonal $S$-invariant nondegenerate planes: the qutrits the tick can
keep to themselves. For every number $n$ of qutrits and every Clifford tick $S\in\Sp(2n,3)$,
\[
A(S)\;=\;n-c(S).
\]
So $A\le n$, $A=n$ exactly when no qutrit is dynamically protected, and $A$ is never $1$. Moreover
$c(S)=\sum_{\lambda=\pm1}\bigl(m_1(\lambda)/2+m_2(\lambda)\bigr)+m_1^{\F_9}(x^2+1)$ is read off the Jordan form.
\end{result}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
In any split, $a\le c$ planes are invariant and the others meet their image at most in a line, so $E\ge n-a\ge n-c$.
For the reverse inequality, remove a maximal invariant family and decompose the rest orthogonally by the
characteristic polynomial and the unipotent and unitary Jordan structure. In each indecomposable piece, a Lagrangian
that is also Lagrangian for $\omega(\cdot,(S+S^{-1})\cdot)$ and transverse to its image (or meeting it in one
eigenvector) gives a split in which every qutrit meets its own image. The Lagrangian is built from a
Taussky--Zassenhaus graph, a Cayley-transform parity subspace, an eigenvector reduction, or a Hermitian real form.
\end{proof}

In the optimal description each protected qutrit exports nothing, and each unprotected one exports \emph{exactly one}
of its two trits: the plane of every unprotected qutrit meets its own image in exactly a line. Theorem~4.7 prices a
forgotten record at two trits. The best choice of subsystems halves that price: one trit, and by Landauer's principle
$k_BT\ln3$ per unprotected qutrit per tick, is the dissipation no description avoids. \tierB

The theorem is checked on every conjugacy class for $n\le5$ ($20$, $74$, $278$ and $940$ classes). For large $n$, a
random tick protects $0.7255\ldots$ qutrits on average (exactly $0.725322$, $0.725518$, $0.725535$ for $n=3,4,5$) and
has the maximal arrow with probability about $0.45$. So a typical dynamics loses exactly one trit per qutrit per tick, less a bounded number of protected qutrits.
The same law holds, class by class, for qubits through $n=5$ (not covered by the proof, which uses odd characteristic)
and for $5$- and $7$-dits. It is a law of Clifford dynamics, not a special property of qutrits.
\tierC\ (\cert{w33\_pass11188\_exact\_arrow\_by\_class.py}, \cert{w33\_pass11199\_arrow\_theorem.py},
\cert{w33\_pass11200\_arrow\_universality.py})

Time reversal is itself not one operation. The anti-symplectic maps with $\theta^2=+1$ are complex conjugation in
suitable subsystems. Those with $\theta^2=-1$ (Kramers type, $T^2$ the parity) exist only for an even number of qutrits
and are local only by exchanging them. For two qutrits they are exactly the $36$ reflections of $W(E_6)$.
\tierC\ (\cert{w33\_pass11192\_time\_reversal\_mereology.py})
